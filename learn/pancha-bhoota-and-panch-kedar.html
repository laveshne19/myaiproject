The element temples and the Panch Kedar

The Pancha Bhoota Sthalams

Five temples in the south, one for each element, where Shiva is worshipped as that element itself.

Ekambareswarar — Earth (पृथ्वी)

Kanchipuram, Tamil Nadu

The linga is of sand. Parvati worshipped it under a mango tree and, when the Kampai river rose to sweep it away, embraced it with her own body rather than let it dissolve. The tree is still there.

Jambukeswarar — Water (जल)

Thiruvanaikaval, Tiruchirappalli, Tamil Nadu

An underground spring keeps the sanctum permanently wet; water seeps around the linga no matter the season. A spider and an elephant, both devotees, worshipped here — and quarrelled about how.

Arunachaleswarar — Fire (अग्नि)

Tiruvannamalai, Tamil Nadu

The hill itself is the linga — the column of fire from the Lingodbhava story. On Karthigai Deepam a cauldron of ghee is lit on the summit and burns for days, visible for miles. Ramana Maharshi never left it.

Srikalahasteeswara — Air (वायु)

Srikalahasti, Andhra Pradesh

A lamp inside the windowless sanctum flickers constantly. Named for a spider (sri), a serpent (kala) and an elephant (hasti) who each worshipped here in their own way and each destroyed the others’ offerings, until Shiva accepted all three.

Chidambaram Nataraja — Ether (आकाश)

Chidambaram, Tamil Nadu

Behind the golden Nataraja hangs a curtain, and behind the curtain — nothing. The Chidambara Rahasya, the secret of Chidambaram: the shrine of space is empty on purpose, and it is shown to you.

The Panch Kedar

Fleeing the Pandavas, Shiva took the form of a bull and sank into the ground. Five parts surfaced across the Garhwal Himalaya, and each became a shrine.

  • Kedarnath — The hump. Rudraprayag, 3,583 m
  • Tungnath — The arms. Rudraprayag, 3,680 m — the highest Shiva temple in the world
  • Rudranath — The face. Chamoli, 3,600 m — reached only on foot
  • Madhyamaheshwar — The navel and stomach. Garhwal, 3,490 m
  • Kalpeshwar — The matted hair. Urgam valley, 2,200 m — open all year
