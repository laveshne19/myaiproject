The Poison Nobody Else Would Drink

हलाहल

The gods were losing. They had aged, they had been defeated, and the only remedy was amrita — the nectar of immortality — which lay at the bottom of the ocean of milk. They could not churn it alone, so they made an alliance with the demons they had been fighting, and promised to share.

They used Mount Mandara as the churning rod and Vasuki, the great serpent, as the rope. Vishnu became a tortoise to hold the mountain steady. The gods took one end, the demons the other, and they pulled for an age.

What came up first was not nectar. It was Halahala — a poison so virulent that the sky darkened and creatures began dying at the edges of the world simply from its presence. Both armies dropped the rope and ran. The alliance, the ambition, the whole enterprise — abandoned in an instant, because the first thing that surfaced was the worst thing.

Shiva walked to the ocean, gathered the poison in His palm and drank it. Parvati, watching, moved fast and pressed His throat so it could travel no further. It stopped there. His throat turned blue, and He has been Neelkanth ever since.

Then the gods and demons came back and finished the churning, and got their nectar.

What it asks of you

Somebody has to take in what the group produced and refuses to own — the bad quarter, the family secret, the mistake nobody will sign. That person is not a martyr and should not become one. Notice what Parvati does: she does not stop Him from drinking, and she does not let it reach His heart. Take it in if you must. Do not let it go all the way down. And do not expect thanks; the churning simply resumes.
