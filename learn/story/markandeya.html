The Boy Who Held On to the Linga

मार्कण्डेय

Mrikandu and Marudmati were childless, and Shiva offered them a choice: a dull son who would live a long life, or a brilliant son who would die at sixteen. They chose the brilliant one.

Markandeya grew up knowing the date. He spent his childhood in devotion, and on the appointed day he went to the Shiva temple and put his arms around the linga and did not let go.

Yama came with his noose. He threw it, and it fell around the boy and around the linga together.

Shiva burst out of the stone. He struck Yama down — that is why He is Kalantaka, the ender of death — and Markandeya was granted to remain sixteen forever.

What it asks of you

The parents were told the terms and chose depth over duration, which is a harder decision than the story admits. But the line that matters is the boy’s: when the thing he had always known was coming finally arrived, he held on to what he had been practising with. He did not suddenly become spiritual at the end. He had been putting his arms around that stone for years. Whatever you will need to hold in the worst hour is the thing you should be holding now, on an ordinary day, when nothing is wrong.
