Six Mothers, Six Faces

कार्तिकेय

Tarakasura could be killed only by a son of Shiva. Shiva was in meditation, Sati was dead, and there was no son.

After Parvati married Him, Shiva’s fire was so intense that no womb could hold it. Agni carried it and could not bear it. Ganga carried it and could not bear it. It was finally set down in a reed forest, and six Krittika stars nursed the six sparks — and Parvati, embracing all six children at once, made them one body with six faces.

He was born with a single purpose, and He fulfilled it young: Tarakasura fell to Him, and He became Devasenapati, commander of the armies of the gods.

Later, when Ganesha won the race around the world by circling their parents instead of the earth, Kartikeya left in anger and went south, to the hills, and stayed. In Tamil Nadu He is Murugan, and the hills are full of Him.

What it asks of you

Some people are born pointed at one thing, and everything about them — the six mothers, the strange gestation, the early war — is arranged around it. That is a gift and it is also a narrowness. The story does not end at the victory; it ends with a brother who felt overlooked and walked away, and became beloved somewhere else. Being made for a purpose does not exempt you from the family you were made in.
