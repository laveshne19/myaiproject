The Fifth Head, and What It Cost

काल भैरव

Brahma had five heads, and with the fifth he began to speak as though he were the supreme being — claiming worship, instructing Shiva.

From Shiva’s anger, Bhairava manifested, and with a fingernail severed the fifth head.

And then the story does something the reader does not expect. Killing a brahmin — and Brahma is the brahmin of brahmins — is the gravest offence there is, and the skull stuck to Bhairava’s hand and would not fall. He wandered the worlds as a beggar with that skull as his bowl, for years, unable to be free of it. Even a righteous killing by a form of God incurred the debt.

Only at Kashi did the skull finally drop. The place is Kapal Mochan. Bhairava stayed on as the guardian of the city, and is said to hold its keys still.

What it asks of you

The tradition could have ended this story at the beheading and called it justice. It does not. Being right does not delete the cost of what being right required you to do — and the story insists you carry it visibly, as a bowl in your hand, and go somewhere to set it down. There is a reason the guardian of the holiest city is someone who did a terrible necessary thing and paid for it in public.
