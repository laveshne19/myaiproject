The Three Cities and the Single Arrow

त्रिपुर दहन

Three sons of the demon Taraka performed austerities and earned three flying cities — one of gold in the sky, one of silver in the air, one of iron on the earth. The boon was carefully worded: the cities could be destroyed only by a single arrow, at the single moment in a thousand years when all three aligned.

For a long age they were unbeatable, and they were also, at first, righteous — devotees of Shiva. Then power did what power does, and they became unbearable.

The gods came to Shiva. He agreed, and they built Him a chariot worthy of the task: the earth as the chariot, the sun and moon as its wheels, Brahma as charioteer, Mount Meru as the bow, Vasuki as the bowstring, Vishnu himself as the arrow. The whole of creation assembled into one weapon.

Shiva stepped up, drew, waited for the alignment — and the texts say He smiled. And the three cities were ash.

He never released the arrow. The gods had assumed the difficulty was in the force required. The difficulty was in the timing, and He was the only one still enough to wait for it.

What it asks of you

You will spend most of your life assembling the chariot — the resources, the skills, the allies — because the visible problem looks like a problem of power. It usually is not. The three cities are the three states you cycle through: waking, dreaming, sleeping; or ambition, fantasy, numbness. They line up rarely, and when they do, one clear act ends all three. Build the bow. Then have the patience not to fire it early.
