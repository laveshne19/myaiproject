Ravana, the Atmalinga, and the Boy Who Would Not Wait

आत्मलिंग

Ravana was Shiva’s greatest devotee — he had composed the Shiva Tandava Stotram beneath Kailash while pinned under the mountain by Shiva’s toe, and sung until Shiva forgave him. Now he performed austerities to win the Atmalinga, the very self of Shiva, to take to Lanka, which would have made Lanka invincible.

Shiva gave it, with one condition: it must not be set on the ground before he reaches home. Wherever it is set down, it stays.

The gods panicked. Vishnu obscured the sun so that dusk seemed to fall, and Ravana — devout, disciplined — had to perform his evening prayers. Ganesha appeared as a small cowherd boy. Ravana asked him to hold the linga for a moment. The boy agreed on a condition of his own: he would call three times, and if Ravana did not return, he would put it down.

He called three times fast. Ravana came running. The linga was on the ground at Gokarna, and it did not move again — not when Ravana pulled at it with all the strength that had lifted Kailash.

What it asks of you

Ravana loses this one for the most sympathetic reason in the epic: he stopped to pray. His devotion was real; his discipline was real; the boon was real. What undid him was that he handed the thing he had earned to someone else for a moment, because he trusted the moment. Do not put the one irreplaceable thing in another person’s hands while you attend to something urgent. And notice: the gods did not defeat him. They arranged for his own virtue to cost him.
