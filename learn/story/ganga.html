Catching a River in Your Hair

गंगावतरण

Sixty thousand sons of King Sagara had been burned to ash by a sage’s glance, and their souls could not move on. Only the celestial Ganga, brought down to touch that ash, could release them.

The family tried. Sagara failed. His son failed. His grandson failed. Finally Bhagiratha, the great-grandson, left his kingdom entirely and performed austerities for a thousand years until Brahma agreed to release the river.

And then Bhagiratha understood the problem. Ganga falling from heaven would not bless the earth; she would split it open. The force that had been prayed for so long would arrive as a catastrophe.

So he began again — this time worshipping Shiva. And Shiva agreed to stand in her path.

Ganga came down proud, meaning to sweep Him away with her. She entered His matted hair and could not find the way out. She wandered in that forest of locks for years. When she finally emerged she was a river a human being could bathe in, and she followed Bhagiratha across the plains to the ash of his ancestors, and they were released.

What it asks of you

This story is about three generations of failure and a fourth that succeeded by asking a different question. The first three asked how to get the blessing. Bhagiratha asked how to survive it. Grace, money, sudden success, the thing you prayed for — arriving at full force, it breaks the ground it lands on. Something has to slow it down first: a discipline, a teacher, an ordinary boring routine. Then it becomes a river you can drink.
