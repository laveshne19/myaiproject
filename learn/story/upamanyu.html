The Boy Who Wanted Milk

उपमन्यु

Upamanyu was a poor boy who had once tasted milk at a relative’s house and afterward cried for it. His mother had none, and she gave him flour mixed with water and told him it was milk, and he knew it was not.

She told him: only Shiva can give you milk. So the boy went into the forest and began austerities with the Om Namah Shivaya mantra, a child alone, for as long as it took.

Shiva came — but disguised as Indra, and He began to speak against Shiva. Why worship an ash-smeared beggar who lives in a cremation ground with ghosts, when Indra, king of heaven, is standing here offering you anything you want?

The boy did not argue. He prepared to end his life rather than listen to Shiva insulted in his presence.

Shiva dropped the disguise, and gave him an ocean of milk that has no end.

What it asks of you

Every serious devotion is tested by a better offer delivered in a reasonable voice. The test is not whether you can defeat the argument — the boy never answered it. It is whether the thing you love is negotiable. This is also, quietly, a story about a hungry child and a mother with nothing to give, and the tradition does not make that metaphorical. He wanted milk. He got milk.
