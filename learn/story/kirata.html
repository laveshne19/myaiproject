The Hunter Who Beat Arjuna

किरातार्जुनीय

Arjuna went to the Himalayas to win the Pashupatastra, Shiva’s weapon. He performed austerities standing on one leg for months.

A wild boar charged him. He shot it. At the same instant an arrow from a tribal hunter struck it too. The boar fell, and the hunter claimed the kill.

Arjuna — the greatest archer alive, a man used to being the greatest archer alive — argued, then fought. He shot everything he had. The hunter absorbed it. Arjuna’s arrows ran out. He attacked with the bow itself, then with his hands, and was beaten to the ground by a forest tribal with no weapons of note.

Broken, he made a small clay linga and offered a garland of flowers to it, and then looked up and saw the garland around the hunter’s neck.

What it asks of you

The thing standing between you and what you are trying to earn is frequently the examiner. Arjuna could not have received that weapon while he still believed he was the best; the fight was not an obstacle to the boon, it was the boon being administered. When something keeps beating you and you cannot understand how, consider that it may be on your side.
