The Boy at the Door

गणेश जन्म

Parvati wanted to bathe undisturbed. Nandi guarded the door, but Nandi belonged to Shiva, and would let Shiva through. So she took the turmeric paste from her own body and made a boy out of it and gave him life, and told him: nobody comes in.

Shiva came home. The boy stopped Him. Shiva explained who He was. The boy did not care; he had one instruction from the only person he had ever known.

Shiva sent the ganas; the boy defeated them. He sent the gods; the boy defeated them too. And then Shiva, who does not lose, severed the boy’s head.

Parvati came out. What she said is not recorded gently: she would end the universe. Shiva sent the ganas north with an order — bring the head of the first living thing you find. They returned with an elephant’s head.

He fixed it to the boy, revived him, and then did something more interesting than the revival: He declared that this son would be worshipped before any other god, in every ritual, forever. Before Shiva Himself.

What it asks of you

A father who did not recognise his own son destroyed him. That happens in ordinary houses, without any severing. What the story then does is unusual: Shiva does not apologise, minimise, or explain that He had good reason. He puts the boy permanently ahead of Himself. That is what repair costs. The god of new beginnings is a god whose own beginning was catastrophic and then repaired — which is precisely why he is the one you invoke when starting something.
