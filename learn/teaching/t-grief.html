Grief Is Meant to Be Finished, Not Managed

When Sati died, Shiva did not process it. He did not accept comfort, or reframe, or get busy. He picked up the body and walked, and the walking was so heavy the world began to come apart.

And the tradition does not treat this as a failure. It marks the fifty-one places where the pieces fell as holy ground, and pilgrims still go to them.

Everything in modern life pressures you to shorten grief — to be functional by Monday, to stop bringing it up. That pressure is not wisdom. Grief that is interrupted does not disappear; it goes underground and runs the rest of your decisions from there.

The promise in the story is specific: what falls from your loss becomes a place of power. Not a metaphor for growth. A place other people will come to for strength, because you went all the way through.

Practice

Give grief an appointment rather than a leash — thirty minutes, a real place, no phone. Grief that is scheduled stops ambushing you.
