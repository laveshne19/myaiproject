How to worship Shiva at home

None of this is required. Shiva accepted a bilva leaf from a hunter who did not know he was worshipping. But if you want a form to hold, this is the ordinary household form.

1. Snana — bathe

Bathe before worship. Clean clothes. Face east or north if you can. This is not superstition; it is a signal to your own attention that something different is starting.

2. Deepa — the lamp

Light a ghee or oil lamp. A single wick is enough. Light it first and put it out last.

3. Sankalpa — the resolve

Say, in your own language, who you are and what this worship is for. Out loud, once. Vagueness here is why so much practice goes nowhere.

4. Abhishek — the pouring

Pour over the linga in this order: water, milk, curd, ghee, honey, sugar (panchamrit), then water again to close. Pour slowly. Chant Om Namah Shivaya throughout. If you have only water, use only water — this is genuinely fine.

5. Bilva patra

Offer bilva leaves with the smooth side down, three lobes intact and unbroken. Even one is sufficient. Bilva is the one offering the texts will not let you substitute.

6. Bhasma & chandan

Three horizontal lines of vibhuti on the forehead — the tripundra — and sandal paste. Ash for what ends, sandalwood for what cools.

7. Naivedya — the offering

Fruit, milk, or anything satvik you would eat yourself. Do not offer what you would not eat.

8. Aarti

Om Jai Shiv Omkara, lamp circling clockwise. Then Karpura Gauram to close.

9. Japa & silence

108 repetitions of Om Namah Shivaya on a rudraksha mala. Then sit still for as long as you can. The silence after is the actual worship; the rest was preparation.

What to offer, and what not to

OfferingWhy
✓ Bilva (bael) leavesThe single most beloved offering. Three lobes, unbroken, smooth side down.
✓ Water & milkThe essential abhishek. Cold water in summer especially — He drank poison; the tradition keeps trying to cool Him.
✓ Bhasma / vibhutiSacred ash. Three lines on the forehead.
✓ White flowers, dhatura, aakDatura and calotropis are His — plants no other deity is offered.
✓ Rudraksha, bhang, honey, sugarcaneAll traditional and all accepted.
✕ Tulsi leavesTulsi is Vishnu’s. The Puranas tell of Vrinda, whose devotion made her sacred to Vishnu; she is not offered to Shiva.
✕ Ketaki (kewda) flowerCursed by Shiva for falsely witnessing Brahma’s claim to have reached the top of the pillar of fire.
✕ Turmeric & kumkum on the lingaHaldi and kumkum are associated with saubhagya and the Goddess; the linga receives bhasma and chandan instead. (Kumkum is offered to Parvati beside Him.)
✕ Coconut water for abhishekCoconut is offered whole and broken as prasad, but its water is not traditionally used for abhishek in most lineages.
✕ Champa & broken riceChampa is cursed in the Shiva Purana; akshat offered to Shiva should be whole, unbroken grains.

Regional lineages differ, sometimes sharply. If your family does it another way, your family is not wrong — follow the tradition you were given.
